1. Purpose

serves people online. This policy sets one standard for everything we publish, so people can find it, read it, trust it, and use it. It covers AI and search readiness, accessibility, security, usability, and readability.

We adopt the ScanGov Standards as our baseline. They are open, public, and based on law and web guidelines. The standards change over time, and we adopt the changes (Appendix A). The baseline is not the whole job. Each section below also lists what we do beyond the baseline.

2. Scope

This policy covers:

  • Our website and every page on it
  • Every subdomain and microsite we own or control
  • Our mobile and web apps
  • Sites that vendors build, host, or run for us
  • What we post on other platforms, as far as the platform allows

It applies to every employee, contractor, and vendor who writes, builds, or publishes for these properties.

3. Policy statement

For every property in scope, commits to the following. Each category has four parts: the outcome we want, the baseline we measure against, what we do beyond the baseline, and how we check.

3.1 AI and search readiness

Outcome

People can find our information with the search engine or AI assistant they already use.

Baseline

Every property meets the ScanGov botability standards. Crawlers can reach it. It has a valid robots file and sitemap. Every page names its canonical address. The main content is in the HTML. Structured data names the organization.

What we do

  • We let well-behaved crawlers in. We block one only for a documented security reason.
  • We publish content as web pages, not as files to download.
  • We keep sitemaps and structured data current when the site changes.
  • We write links and page titles that say where they lead.

How we check

We check every property against the baseline.

3.2 Accessibility

Outcome

People with disabilities can use every service we publish, with the assistive technology they choose.

Baseline

Every property meets the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) and the ScanGov accessibility standards.

What we do

  • We test with a keyboard alone and with a screen reader before every launch or major change.
  • We test with people with disabilities when we design or redesign a service.
  • We buy accessible products. Every contract requires a current Accessibility Conformance Report and a plan to fix what the product does not support.
  • We publish an accessibility statement with a way to report a barrier. We respond to every report within business days.
  • We train everyone who publishes content or code.
  • If we cannot fix something by its deadline, we offer an accessible alternative on request.
  • This section carries out the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Title II web rule (28 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) part 35, subpart H).
  • This section carries out Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act (36 CFR part 1194).
  • This section carries out the state accessibility law and any local ordinance that applies to us.

How we check

We check every property against the baseline. A keyboard and screen reader review at each policy review.

3.3 Security

Outcome

People can trust that our sites are ours and that what they share with us stays safe.

Baseline

Every property serves every page over HTTPS and meets the ScanGov security standards. These include the response headers that the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) recommends and a public security.txt file.

What we do

  • We patch software and dependencies on a schedule. We remove what we no longer use.
  • We review application security on a schedule, from inside and outside perspectives.
  • We keep a channel open for security reports and answer every one.
  • We limit admin access to what each role needs, and we require multi-factor authentication for it.
  • We fix a security failure we find within days.
  • We serve our main site from a .gov domain, or another sponsored top-level domain, where we qualify.

How we check

We check every property against the baseline. A security review at each policy review.

3.4 Usability and performance

Outcome

People can finish the task they came for, on any device and any connection.

Baseline

Every page meets the ScanGov usability standards. These include the Core Web Vitals thresholds, a unique title and description, a mobile viewport, and sharing tags.

What we do

  • We design for mobile first and test on real devices.
  • We do user research and task-based testing for every new or redesigned service.
  • We use analytics and feedback to find the tasks people fail. We fix those first.

How we check

We check every property against the baseline. Task-based testing before launch.

3.5 Readability

Outcome

People understand what we publish the first time they read it.

Baseline

Every page reads at the level or lower, measured with the Automated Readability Index (ARI), the ScanGov readability standard.

What we do

  • We use short sentences, common words, active voice, and headings that say what follows.
  • We spell out acronyms the first time. We define jargon or cut it.
  • We review content before we publish it. We train everyone who writes for the public.
  • We offer content in the languages the people we serve use.
  • This section carries out the Plain Writing Act of 2010 and Office of Management and Budget (OMB) memorandum M-23-22.

How we check

We check every property against the baseline. Editorial review before publishing.

4. Roles

  • owns this policy, keeps the accessibility statement current, reviews results, and reports progress to leadership at each policy review.
  • Content authors write in plain language, add alt text and captions, and use headings and link text well.
  • Developers and web administrators keep templates, code, headers, and hosting in line with this policy, and fix failures.
  • Procurement writes the ScanGov Standards into every request for proposal and contract, and requires an Accessibility Conformance Report for any platform we buy.
  • Vendors deliver work that meets this policy, and fix failures found in acceptance testing before launch.

5. Measurement

We measure against the baseline, not with statements. will:

  • Check every property in scope against the baseline at each policy review and when a property changes.
  • Track progress for each property. A property that falls below the baseline is a defect.
  • Review results at each policy review and publish a summary.
  • Fix the barriers that block a service first.
