1. Purpose

serves people online. This policy sets one standard for everything we publish, so people can find it, read it, trust it, and use it. It covers accessibility, AI and search readiness, security, usability, and plain language.

We adopt the ScanGov Standards as our floor. They are open, public, and based on law and web guidelines. A scan checks them. A failing scan is a defect. The standards change over time, and we adopt the changes (Appendix A). The scan is the floor, not the whole job. Each section below also lists what we do beyond the scan.

2. Scope

This policy covers:

  • Our website, , and every page on it
  • Every subdomain and microsite we own or control
  • Our mobile and web apps
  • Sites that vendors build, host, or run for us
  • What we post on other platforms, as far as the platform allows

It applies to every employee, contractor, and vendor who writes, builds, or publishes for these properties.

3. Policy statement

For every property in scope, commits to the following. Each category has four parts: the outcome we want, the floor a scan measures, what we do beyond the scan, and how we check.

3.1 Accessibility

Outcome. People with disabilities can use every service we publish, with the assistive technology they choose.

Floor. Every property meets the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) and the ScanGov accessibility standards.

What we do.

  • We test with a keyboard alone and with a screen reader before every launch or major change.
  • We test with people with disabilities when we design or redesign a service.
  • We buy accessible products. Every contract requires a current Accessibility Conformance Report and a plan to fix what the product does not support.
  • We publish an accessibility statement with a way to report a barrier. We respond within business days.
  • We train everyone who publishes content or code.
  • If we cannot fix something by its deadline, we offer an accessible alternative on request.
  • This section carries out the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Title II web rule (28 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) part 35, subpart H).
  • This section carries out Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act (36 CFR part 1194).
  • This section carries out the state accessibility law and any local ordinance that applies to us.

How we check. A scan on the scan schedule. A keyboard and screen reader review on the policy review cycle.

3.2 AI and search readiness

Outcome. People can find our information with the search engine or AI assistant they already use.

Floor. Every property meets the ScanGov botability standards. Crawlers can reach it. It has a valid robots file and sitemap. Every page names its canonical address. The main content is in the HTML. Structured data names the organization.

What we do.

  • We let well-behaved crawlers in. We block one only for a documented security reason.
  • We publish content as web pages, not as files to download.
  • We keep sitemaps and structured data current when the site changes.
  • We write links and page titles that say where they lead.

How we check. A scan on the scan schedule.

3.3 Security

Outcome. People can trust that our sites are ours and that what they share with us stays safe.

Floor. Every property serves every page over HTTPS and meets the ScanGov security standards. These include the response headers that the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) recommends and a public security.txt file.

What we do.

  • We patch software and dependencies on a schedule. We remove what we no longer use.
  • We review application security on a schedule, from inside and outside perspectives.
  • We keep a channel open for security reports and answer every one.
  • We limit admin access to what each role needs, and we require multi-factor authentication for it.
  • We fix a failure a scan reports within days.
  • We serve our main site from a .gov domain, or another sponsored top-level domain, where we qualify.

How we check. A scan on the scan schedule. A security review on the policy review cycle.

3.4 Usability and performance

Outcome. People can finish the task they came for, on any device and any connection.

Floor. Every page meets the ScanGov usability standards. These include the Core Web Vitals thresholds, a unique title and description, a mobile viewport, and sharing tags.

What we do.

  • We design for mobile first and test on real devices.
  • We do user research and task-based testing for every new or redesigned service.
  • We use analytics and feedback to find the tasks people fail. We fix those first.

How we check. A scan on the scan schedule. Task-based testing before launch.

3.5 Plain language

Outcome. People understand what we publish the first time they read it.

Floor. Every page reads at grade or below on the Automated Readability Index (ARI), the ScanGov readability standard.

What we do.

  • We use short sentences, common words, active voice, and headings that say what follows.
  • We spell out acronyms the first time. We define jargon or cut it.
  • We review content before we publish it. We train everyone who writes for the public.
  • We offer content in the languages the people we serve use.
  • This section carries out the Plain Writing Act of 2010 and Office of Management and Budget (OMB) memorandum M-23-22.

How we check. A scan on the scan schedule. Editorial review before publishing.
