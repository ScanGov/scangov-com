Writing a website redesign request for proposal (RFP) means turning accessibility, security, performance, and search and AI readiness into requirements a vendor can be held to. This builder does that for you. It writes the ScanGov Standards into section 3 of a complete RFP, links every requirement to the standard that defines it, and adds contract terms so compliance is verified by scans rather than promised. Free, no signup, and nothing you enter is stored.

ScanGov checks government websites against these same standards, so a vendor who meets the RFP will also pass the public scorecard your residents and your council can see.

See how your current site scores first

Before you write requirements for the new site, look up the old one. ScanGov has already scanned most US government websites and publishes a scorecard for each. The failing standards on your current site are the ones to put in the RFP.

What the RFP requires

Section 3 of the generated RFP covers the four categories ScanGov scores, with each requirement linked to its standard and guidance:

  • Accessibility: WCAG 2.1 AA and all 66 ScanGov accessibility standards, from color contrast to touch target size.
  • Botability: the site can be crawled and understood by search engines and AI assistants, with content in the HTML rather than only after JavaScript runs.
  • Security: HTTPS, HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS), a content security policy, security.txt, and the other headers the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP) recommend.
  • Usability: Core Web Vitals thresholds, plain-language readability, page titles and descriptions, mobile viewport, and social sharing tags.

The rest of the document is a conventional RFP skeleton you can edit: background, functional requirements, content management system (CMS) and integrations, design, deliverables, timeline, proposal requirements, evaluation criteria, and contract terms.
