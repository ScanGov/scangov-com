About readability scan

Plain language helps people find answers fast. This scan loads a page and finds its main text. It scores that text with two formulas: the Automated Readability Index (ARI) and Flesch-Kincaid. A page passes at grade 8 or below. Strong content teams aim for grade 6. The scan is free, needs no signup, and shows results on this page.

Method

The scan uses the public equations for the Automated Readability Index (ARI) and the Flesch–Kincaid readability tests. ARI counts characters, words, and sentences. Flesch–Kincaid also counts syllables. The scan reviews the page and pulls out the main content. Some parts of a page, like headings, have no punctuation. The scan adds a period to the end of each one, so the formulas grade all the content fairly.

About readability

Readability measures how easy text is to understand. It looks at words and layout. Formulas like Flesch-Kincaid and ARI guess a reading grade. ARI uses letters per word and words per sentence to give a U.S. school grade. Grade 8 means eighth grade. Simple words, short sentences, clear design, and easy fonts help people read.

Why it matters

People get what they need the first time, without calling or visiting. Short sentences and common words help every reader. That includes people who read in a second language and people in a hurry.

What grade to aim for

Grade 8 is a common goal for a general audience. It is the level ScanGov scores against. Teams with strong plain language habits aim for grade 6. Aim for 6 on the pages people need most, like applying, paying, and reporting. Use 8 as the lowest bar.

The formulas measure only word and sentence length. So the quickest wins are simple ones. Write one idea per sentence. Pick common words over formal ones. Put the action first. Use headings and lists in place of long paragraphs. The federal plain language guidelines and digital.gov plain language tips have more.

What the scan checks

The readability standard looks at the main text of a page. It skips menus and boilerplate. Then it runs two public formulas: ARI and Flesch-Kincaid. A page passes when its ARI rounds to grade 8 or below.

Readability is its own indicator. ScanGov scores it next to accessibility, botability, security, and usability. Plain language work shows up as its own grade. This scan uses the same formulas and pass rule as the ScanGov scan. It also shows the exact text it scored.

Track it over time

Rewriting many pages takes months. To see progress, measure every page, again and again. The ScanGov monitoring product scans every page. Its readability section lists pages above the target grade. Your team can work through the list, watch it shrink, and catch new text that slips.
