Plain language is a legal requirement for federal websites and the standard most state and local governments hold themselves to. This checker fetches a page, pulls out the main content, and scores it with the two formulas ScanGov uses across every government site it scans: Automated Readability Index and Flesch-Kincaid. A page passes at grade 8 or below; mature content teams aim for 6. Free, no signup, results on the page.

How your whole site scores

ScanGov has scanned most US government websites and publishes a public scorecard for each on scangov.org, with readability among the usability checks. Browse the usability rankings to find your site, and read more about government website readability.

About

Readability measures how easy text is to understand by looking at words and layout. Formulas like Flesch-Kincaid and the Automated Readability Index estimate a reading grade level. ARI uses the average number of characters per word and words per sentence to give a US school grade; 8 means eighth grade. Simple words, short sentences, clear design, and legible fonts make text easier to read.

Why it matters

Residents get what they need the first time, without calling or visiting. As of September 2026, about six in ten government homepages ScanGov has scanned meet the grade-8 standard and about one in three meet the grade-6 target.
