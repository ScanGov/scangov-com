What is a good bot?

  • It visits slowly and follows the rules in your robots.txt file.
  • It gives its name on every visit, so you can see who is there.
  • It has a page that explains who runs it and why.

About

This tool checks whether ScanGovBot can reach your website. It looks at three things:

  • Your robots.txt file, to see if ScanGovBot is allowed in and how long it is asked to wait between visits
  • A basic request, like the one your browser makes
  • A full browser, in case the basic request is turned away

If we cannot get in, we cannot scan your site. Other good bots are probably being turned away too.

This tool only tells you about ScanGovBot. Another good bot may get a different result on the same site. If we were blocked, check your robots.txt file and your firewall, WAF, and CDN bot settings. The block may be a setting no one chose.

Common issues

  • Your robots.txt file tells ScanGovBot to stay out, or asks it to wait too long between visits
  • A firewall blocks visits that do not come from a person
  • The server sends back an error, such as 403 or 503
  • The site has a security certificate problem
  • The site takes too long to answer

Access is nuanced

A failed check does not always mean your site blocks every good bot. Most sites let some bots in and keep others out in these ways:

  • robots.txt: your file can name one bot and tell it to stay out, while the rest are welcome.
  • Allow lists: the site lets in a few well-known bots and blocks the rest.
  • Block lists: one bot is named and blocked, while the others still get in.
  • Firewall defaults: many firewalls and CDNs block bots they do not recognize, even if no one asked them to.
  • Traffic limits: a bot you allow can still be shut out for visiting too often, or for the network it comes from.
